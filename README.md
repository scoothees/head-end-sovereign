# 🧠 HOW IT CONNECTS TO SOVEREIGN

The hidden archive is framed as a **SOVEREIGN historical-response model** recovered from the network.

SOVEREIGN studies the 2016 incident because it contains exactly the questions the system is obsessed with:

- How quickly should humans intervene?
- When does uncertainty justify irreversible action?
- What happens when authority is divided?
- Does prevention outperform emergency automation?
- How much public trust is lost when institutions communicate poorly?
- Can safety systems prevent a crisis without surrendering control to a central system?

Your counterfactual choices can therefore create **legacy effects in the main campaign**.

Examples:

| GORILLA WORLD LEGACY | POSSIBLE SOVEREIGN-WORLD EFFECT |
|---|---|
| Child goes somewhere else / breach prevented | Stronger prevention doctrine, Human Control and Safety |
| Transparent coordinated response | Higher Public Trust / Cooperation; can open `Common Cause` |
| Strong manual safeguards | Can open `Human Override` |
| Heavy crisis automation | Higher SOVEREIGN Reach; can open `Terms and Conditions` |
| High-quality evidence trail | More Evidence available to later investigators |
| Poor coordination / high danger | Lower Safety and weaker institutional trust |

Later SOVEREIGN roles can explicitly reference the most recent Gorilla World archive legacy.

# 📚 HISTORICAL GROUNDING

The playable archive is **alternate history**, but its baseline uses public information released by the Cincinnati Zoo.

The documented baseline incorporated into the game includes:

- On **May 28, 2016**, the Cincinnati Zoo said a **4-year-old boy** crossed a public barrier at Gorilla World at about **4 p.m.** and fell into the exhibit moat.
- The zoo said **two female gorillas were recalled**, while Harambe remained in the yard with the child.
- The zoo's **Dangerous Animal Response Team** killed Harambe because staff considered the child to be in imminent danger.
- The zoo later said the drop was about **15 feet** and that the moat contained about **one foot of water**.
- The zoo stated that tranquilization was not considered an immediate solution because it could take several minutes and the dart impact could agitate the gorilla.
- The child was transported for medical care and was reported released that night.
- Gorilla World later reopened with a **taller barrier, knotted rope netting and surveillance cameras**.
- The zoo later cited a USDA statement saying the pre-incident barrier had been considered compliant during previous inspections.

- Cincinnati Zoo — **Cincinnati Zoo Devastated by Death of Beloved Gorilla** (May 29, 2016): https://cincinnatizoo.org/cincinnati-zoo-devastated-by-death-of-beloved-gorilla/

### Counterfactual disclaimer
Everything the player changes — including the child going somewhere else, Harambe surviving, different emergency decisions, injuries, deaths, alternate public reaction, or downstream consequences — is **fictional alternate history**. The game does not claim that any real person would have made those choices, does not identify the child, and does not claim to know Harambe's internal thoughts.
